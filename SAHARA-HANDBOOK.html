1. What Sahara Is — and What It Is Not

Sahara ("सहारा" = support) is a digital support platform built for the Smart India Hackathon (SIH26094) for the Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment. It helps a person who has filed a complaint under the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act stay emotionally anchored during the long, often lonely journey that follows — checking in, taking small steps toward wellbeing, and connecting with human support when it is needed — before a crisis, not after.

What Sahara is

  • A calm, private space to share how you are feeling and see your own wellbeing trend.
  • A guided wellbeing check-in that only suggests risk to a human — it never decides.
  • A multilingual companion (English, हिंदी, ଓଡ଼ିଆ) to talk to, guided by trauma-informed prompts.
  • A safety net for caseworkers: a prioritised queue and a timeline of AI signals vs. human action.
  • A monitoring route for officials: national → state → district views, without leaking private details.

What Sahara is NOT

  • Not a replacement for the NHAA helpline (14566). The helpline stays the first door for reporting and immediate help.
  • Not a clinical diagnosis. It produces "AI-assisted risk estimates," not medical opinions.
  • Not an automatic decision-maker. No action ever happens without a trained human reviewing it.
  • Not your whole support system. It connects you to humans, it does not replace them.

3. The Big Idea: Complaint → Care

Sahara sits deliberately right after the complaint:

  1. Helpline (14566): reporting + immediate help — the first door.
  2. Complaint filed under the SC/ST (PoA) Act — the formal legal step.
  3. Sahara: the quiet, continuous wellbeing support after filing — check-ins, small steps, and informed caseworkers.
  4. Caseworker review: AI signals are reviewed by a person who decides whether to reach out.

In short: the helpline starts the journey; Sahara stays with the person through it.

4. How a Visitor Uses the Website (General Public)

No account is needed. The public site does the explaining.

  1. Open the homepage. You land on a calm mountain-and-sunrise hero reading "Hope Still Rises," with the headline "A safer, kinder tomorrow."
  2. Choose a lane on "How it works": I need support · I'm a caseworker or counsellor · I'm a government partner.
  3. Browse the site: How It Works · For Victims & Families · For Officials · Resources · Contact · About · Privacy.
  4. Use the Resources page: a filterable helpline directory with click-to-call numbers.
  5. Try the floating chat bot: any visitor can talk to the demo companion; a human is behind it.
  6. Quick Exit: use the safety button to leave and clear the screen in one tap.
  7. Switch language: toggle English / हिंदी in the navigation; Odia is on the roadmap.

What to tell a survivor family

You do not need to understand the system to use it. Call 14566, or ask anyone you trust to reach out for you — consent stays yours either way.

5. How a Survivor (Beneficiary) Uses It, Step by Step

The beneficiary portal is the survivor's own private space — "My Sahara." It shows only their own check-in history, never anyone else's.

  1. Sign in. Use the emailed beneficiary account (the demo account is in Section 11). The portal opens on "Your wellbeing, in your hands."
  2. See your trend. A calm chart shows your distress scores over the last 60 days so you can see how you are doing at a glance — narrated in words, not just graphs.
  3. Do a wellbeing check-in. Answer a few short questions (mood, sleep, feeling safe, any recent incident, support received). This feeds only your own record.
  4. Review your support plan. Small, gentle next steps and reflections are offered — never a list of demands.
  5. Talk to Sahara. Open the companion and share how you feel in English, हिंदी, or ଓଡ଼ିଆ. It can also walk you through a check-in conversationally.
  6. See your risk honestly. If the system flags a change, you are told it is an "AI-assisted risk estimate — not a diagnosis," and that a human review happens before any contact.
  7. Request a human. Ask for human support at any moment. A caseworker follows up — nothing automated overrides that.
  8. Delete or pause anytime. One tap. Withdrawing consent never affects the underlying complaint.

Why it feels safe

Only what you consent to share is used: your check-ins and the support record you build. Location and messages are not inputs to the risk engine. You can leave whenever you want.

6. How a Caseworker Uses It, Step by Step

The caseworker console turns a noisy caseload into a quiet, prioritised list.

  1. Open the risk queue. Cases are sorted crisis-first, then by priority score — with a reason for why each case is ranked where it is.
  2. Read the explainability panel. For any case you see the current risk, 7/14/30-day trend, escalation probability, contributing factors, and "why has this case been prioritised?"
  3. Open a case timeline. A single view distinguishes AI events from human actions, so you always know what the machine suggested vs. what someone actually did.
  4. Check crisis chat flags. A panel lists users flagged in the last 24 hours with urgency, sentiment and keywords — never raw conversation text in the dashboard.
  5. Log a human follow-up. When you contact or escalate, record it — this logs a timestamp and action.
  6. Decide with your judgment. Marking follow-up completed never auto-reduces the score or marks someone safe — you decide.

Human-over-AI

The AI ranks and signals; the caseworker decides and acts. The system never auto-contacts on a risk signal.

7. How Officials / Admin Use It, Step by Step

District, state and national admins monitor aggregated, privacy-preserving views.

  1. District officer: sees a focused district view (e.g. Odisha · Khordha) of risk bands and queues.
  2. State admin: sees state-level aggregates across districts — counts per risk band, worsening trends, crisis flags.
  3. National admin: rolls states up into a national picture for monitoring and response planning.
  4. Key detail: dashboards hide raw chat and private narratives, showing only aggregated counts and explainable reasons — no PII flood.
  5. Deep links: officials can drill to a demonstrable case (e.g. DEMO-042) to see the full complaint→care workflow.

Design goal

Give leadership a truthful, decision-ready picture without exposing sensitive personal information.

9. Benefits of Using Sahara

For survivors

  • You are not alone. A quiet companion checks in on you between official appointments — before a crisis, not after.
  • Small steps, real progress. Gentle nudges and reflections you can actually take, in your language.
  • Yours only. A private space showing only your own data; nothing is shared without consent.
  • Leave anytime. Pausing or withdrawing is one tap and never harms your underlying complaint.

For caseworkers

  • See the whole story. Every contact and signal in one honest timeline (AI vs. human).
  • Work the right cases. A crisis-first queue means your judgment, not your inbox, decides.
  • Proof of your care. Follow-ups are logged and visible — no false reassurance.

For officials & government

  • Decision-ready, private-safe. Aggregates and explainable reasons without raw conversations.
  • State-integration friendly. One Stop Centres, helplines and grievance desks can plug in.
  • DPDP-aligned posture. Access is role-limited and minimised-by-design.

The core benefit, in one line

Sahara turns a lonely, intimidating process into a supported, human-checked journey — the machine watches for distress, and a person decides how to help.

Core collections: users, checkins, risk_assessments, alerts, caseworker_actions, chat_sessions, chat_logs, transcripts, audit_logs.

Data flow: Check-in / chat / transcript → scoring & NLP services → MongoDB → distress engine + prioritisation → risk queue / summaries / chat flags → caseworker review → human follow-up (logged, never auto-cleared).

13. Privacy, Ethics & the "Human Decides" Rule

  • Raw phone numbers never stored — only a bcrypt hash.
  • API keys are server-side only — the Claude key lives in the backend, never the browser.
  • Minimised dashboards — queues show case IDs, scores, and reasons, not private conversations; chat flags show keywords/sentiment, not transcripts.
  • No protected characteristics as features — caste/religion/ethnicity are never inputs to the risk model.
  • CORS locked to the frontend origins.
  • Human-over-AI — the engine ranks, suggests, and flags; an authorised counsellor or caseworker decides and acts. Marking follow-up complete never auto-lowers a score.

Honest labels everywhere

"AI-assisted risk estimate. This is not a clinical diagnosis." "Prototype model — deployment would require validation, clinical oversight, bias assessment, security review, and government approval." Every number self-labels; the human is the last word.
